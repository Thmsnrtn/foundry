# Competitor: Mercury
Category: Startup OS / Back-Office Tools
URL: https://mercury.com

## What They Do
Mercury is a fintech banking platform purpose-built for startups, offering business checking/savings accounts, credit cards, venture debt, treasury management, and SAFE fundraising tools. They position themselves as a "financial stack" rather than just a bank, bundling banking with expense management, accounting integrations, and capital products. Over 300K startups use Mercury, and the company reached $650M annualized revenue at a $3.5B valuation.

## Target User
Seed-to-Series C startup founders, finance leads at scaling companies, and operators in SaaS, ecommerce, agencies, VC funds, and crypto. Primarily US-based tech companies that want a modern, API-first banking experience.

## Key Features
- Free business checking and savings with up to $5M FDIC insurance via sweep networks
- Treasury management with up to 3.65% APY on idle cash
- IO business credit card with 1.5% unlimited cashback, no personal guarantee
- Venture debt (up to 48-month term loans for startups with $2M+ raised)
- SAFEs by Mercury: end-to-end SAFE document creation, distribution, tracking
- Expense management with team controls and approval workflows
- Native accounting sync (QuickBooks, Xero, NetSuite)
- AI-powered financial insights and automated bookkeeping
- Full read-write API for custom integrations (Plaid, Stripe)
- Invoice creation, bill pay, and payment processing (no USD fees)
- Account opening in 10 minutes, fully online

## Pricing
- **Free tier**: Checking, savings, credit card, API access, basic tools
- **Plus** ($35/mo): Higher APY, advanced accounting integrations, priority support
- **Pro** ($350/mo): High-volume expense management, dedicated support
- Venture debt and treasury: custom pricing based on company stage
- International wires: $5 + ~1.5% FX markup

## Strengths (vs Foundry)
- Massive adoption (300K+ startups) creates powerful network effects and data advantages
- Financial infrastructure is mission-critical and deeply sticky once adopted
- Expanding beyond banking into a full financial OS (capital, fundraising, accounting)
- Beautiful UX that sets the standard for fintech design
- Free tier removes all friction for early-stage founders

## Weaknesses (vs Foundry)
- Financial-only lens: no coverage of team, compliance, metrics, product, or operations
- No autonomous agents or AI-driven decision-making across the business
- Cannot manage multi-entity structures or complex corporate hierarchies
- No built-in compliance, HR, or operational tooling
- Limited to reactive financial reporting rather than proactive strategic guidance
- Does not provide portfolio-level views for founders running multiple companies
- Integration limitations become apparent as companies scale beyond Series B

## Key Insight
Mercury proves that startup founders will consolidate onto a single platform if it removes friction from a critical workflow. Foundry should study Mercury's "expand from wedge" playbook: they started with banking (the most essential primitive) and are expanding into capital, accounting, and financial intelligence. Foundry's wedge is the autonomous control plane itself -- the ability to see and act across all company functions simultaneously, which Mercury cannot do from a finance-only vantage point.
