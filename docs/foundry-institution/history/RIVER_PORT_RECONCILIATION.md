# The River port: reconciliation onto the campaign line

**Date:** 2026-09-08. **Owner decision:** Option A. The private campaign/frontier
lineage is Foundry; its production database, owner-governed history,
capabilities, evidence, decisions and deployment model are preserved. The River
constitution, the economic-probe kernel, Proof 1 and the owner-facing surfaces
built on `claude/foundry-river-constitution-rvd0mx` (tag `river-line`,
`ccf75da4`, five commits on `master`) are ported onto the frontier tip
`402b1f99` as a **semantic port and reconciliation**, not a Git merge. Where
this line has newer or stronger semantics, the River implementation is adapted
to them. Nothing is duplicated.

This file is the decision record. The slice record is in
`IMPLEMENTATION_SLICES.md`; the doctrine is `../RIVER.md`.

## What the port changed on contact with the frontier (2026-09-08)

The decision table above held. Building against the frontier's guards forced four adaptations, each toward the stronger semantics:

1. **The paid event is the delivery.** `settleFromTheWorld` counts events of one kind and does not net refunds. River's "validated needs a settled payment AND a delivered brief" is therefore sealed as `{event: 'delivery', at_least 1, out_of 'offer_delivered', at_most 25, within 7 days}`. A payment that bounces and is refunded produces no `delivery` event and validates nothing; the delivery is classified by who paid, read from the provider again at reconciliation and dropped, so the owner's own purchase delivered to himself counts for nothing.
2. **The door resolves the act from rows, never from the caller.** The kill switch already refused every effect from an experimental asset "until a hand names the act". `experimentActFor` (standing-intent.ts) fills that shape from three row-based paths: a message is the hand's own planned, guarded, claimed `outbound_actions` row (offers need a live test; deliveries outlive settlement); a refund is an `experiment_fulfilments` row with a refund requested; a placement is the exact params fingerprint of the act the owner approved; a take-down is a withdrawn exposure under the placement act. An ask-first boundary on the asset is answered by the covering act, which is marked used on first use.
3. **Allow is three exact acts under three ask-first boundaries** scoped to the test's asset (`contact_people`, `publish`, `move_money`), not one River grant. The campaign act's parameters are the reviewed recipients and the offer text; the placement act's are the payment link's precise parameters; the refund act covers purchases reported at this exposure and nothing else. Stop revokes all three after taking the link down.
4. **The offer comes down when the test ends.** `withdraw_payment_link` is its own capability (one provider per capability), `stripe_deactivate_payment_link` its handler; the hand takes the link down on settlement and on stop, and what is owed outlives the test: a purchase that slipped in is delivered if the acts stand, refunded if the offer was withdrawn before delivery.

Also: the `nothing_asked_for_this` trigger, `front_loaded_attention` and `one_visit_delivery` structural facts, and `capability_providers`' one-provider-per-capability uniqueness were all met by the rows rather than relaxed. Nothing in migrations 1–283 was changed.
