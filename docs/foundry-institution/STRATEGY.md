# Strategy: what Foundry should become, and what would show it wrong

*Written 28 September 2026, at the owner's request for a strategic roadmap of
50–100 items. Revised the same day with the digital-product handoff of 28
September. The handoff is cited by date only: it names other businesses of the
owner, and this repository is public.*

**This is not a product backlog.** The handoff says it plainly: *do not
translate this into a 100-item backlog or a speculative digital-products
subsystem.* Nothing here is built because it is listed. An item becomes work
only when a slice earns it under `DEVELOPMENT_INSTITUTION.md`: a verified gap,
a governing requirement, the smallest complete change, and tests. Most items
are questions to settle in writing, or events only the world can supply.

**Where it sits.** `ROADMAP.md` is the execution list. This file sits one
level above it. It asks what Foundry should *become*, which ideas are still
unexamined, and what would show each one right or wrong. Items are numbered
S1–S100, and the numbers are stable so later work can cite them ("S38").

**Rules every item obeys.**
- Nothing here authorises spending, publishing, messaging, contacting buyers,
  or changing any account. No item creates authority from capability.
- Every item names the external event or measured quantity that would show it
  right or wrong.
- An item that moves none of OBJECTIVE §6's three measures (recurring
  owner-minutes down, calibration up, cost per decision down) is **machinery**,
  and waits behind an event.

Each item is tagged:
- **[think]**: a doctrine or design question, settled in writing;
- **[code]**: buildable, when a slice earns it;
- **[world]**: needs reality;
- **[owner]**: his decision.

*Status notes* in italics record where later work already touched an item.

## A. What Foundry is for

1. **[think] The thesis as a falsifiable bet.** "Foundry lowers the minimum viable size of an ownable asset." Write down the numbers that would disprove it: `a` does not fall below X minutes a month after N assets, or overhead per asset does not fall as the count rises.
2. **[think] Estimate λ from his behaviour, not by asking.** His value per minute is the pivot of `v > λ·a`. Derive a range from what he declines and delegates, then show him the implied figure to confirm or correct.
3. **[think] The smallest ownable asset in each form.** For a download, a tool and a data brief: the least `v` worth holding once `a` is known.
4. **[think] What Foundry refuses to be.** Not an agency, not a content farm, not a SaaS builder. Each refusal names the mechanism that would drift it there: search-page spam, or a service burden that grows with volume.
5. **[think] Success at 1, 5 and 25 assets.** Attention binds at 1, correlation at 5, overhead and legal surface at 25. Write the first binding constraint at each scale.
6. **[owner] "Operate and allocate", or "originate".** OBJECTIVE §9 (formerly the second §8) leans to operating. Make it a dated choice with a review trigger, not a drift.

## B. Economics and capital allocation

7. **[think] A test is a real option.** Its value is the decision the answer would change. Price each design by what it could reverse, not only by its affordable loss.
8. **[code] One capital account.** Every dollar is in tests, the reserve, running costs, or free, and the four sum to what is held, over one period.
9. **[think] Cognition costed by the decision it serves.** Model spend per decision should fall as calibration rises. Define the curve, then measure it.
10. **[code] A break-even only with its reachability condition.** No break-even figure without "if buyers can reach it" attached, in words.
11. **[think] A harvest policy.** When an asset is stable and its `a` near zero, its cash goes to tests, the reserve, or him. A default rule that he ratifies.
12. **[think] A kill question that raises itself.** An asset below its covenant for N periods raises a decision. It never kills by itself; it makes keeping the asset an active choice.
13. **[think] What Foundry itself costs.** Hosting, models and his minutes, against the portfolio's contribution, in one monthly line.
14. **[world] Four ledgers, from the first real sale on.** The handoff's split: **(1) settled buyer receipts; (2) variable costs and refunds; (3) maintenance and owner attention; (4) shared infrastructure and marketing.** Contribution after (2) serves a marginal decision. A sustainable draw needs (3), (4), taxes and cash timing. The portfolio metric is contribution per hour of owner attention and per dollar at risk. *D4 and D6 hold (1), (2) and part of (3).*
15. **[think] A price is tested by pre-registration.** Seal the test window, price, traffic exclusions, listing version, success signal and stop rule before the listing is live. Change one major variable per test. Never show two prices at once to different people.

## C. Evidence, epistemics and proof

16. **[think] One grade on every figure.** "Measured", "estimated", "owner-entered" and "inferred" are always visible. Commercial claims carry their CE rung, and machinery claims their E rung. *The CE reader exists (handoff A7, `commercial-maturity.ts`), and stops at CE4.*
17. **[code] "Why do I believe this", one tap behind every number:** its rows, source and read date.
18. **[think] The absence doctrine as a checkable property.** "Not read" is never "none". Only a complete read concludes absence.
19. **[think] Reading a listing's result.** The handoff's table becomes the doctrine:

    | Question | Minimum denominator | What it can show | What it cannot |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Can strangers find it? | Eligible listing days; impressions by source | Whether this channel supplied exposure | Market size or lasting rank |
    | Does the page invite purchase? | Qualified visits, attributed purchases, listing version | Conversion for this page and traffic | Usefulness after purchase |
    | Does it work for buyers? | Confirmed use, errors, support, refunds | Whether the promise survives use | Retention from one buyer |
    | Can Foundry carry it? | Message coverage, delivery and remedy paths | Routine care under tested conditions | Seven-day autonomy from simulation |
    | Is it a livelihood component? | Settled receipts and the four ledgers, across months | Contribution and owner time for the period | Wage replacement from one launch month |

    No listing, no test. Impressions without visits test the channel. Visits without orders press on the page, promise or price, but do not say which. One sale is one buyer's willingness, and starts an obligation.
20. **[think] Freshness is part of truth.** Each kind of reading states when it stops being current.
21. **[think] Counterfactual discipline.** For each settled test: did it change a decision? A test that changed nothing was machinery.
22. **[code] The follow-up is sealed with the test:** "if as predicted, then X; if not, then Y".
23. **[world] A lesson is seen twice, in two settings, before it is called a rule.**

## D. Learning and calibration

24. **[code] Calibration on Home,** as a share of graded predictions with its count, even at n = 1.
25. **[think] A lesson has an expiry:** its source, context, contrary evidence, invalidation condition and review date.
26. **[think] Consolidation.** When N episodes share a pattern, propose a rule. He ratifies it, and it is kept with its episodes so it can be unwound.
27. **[code] Reference-class forecasting.** Show the base rate from similar settled tests before sealing, or say that none exist.
28. **[think] Disagreement is signal.** Record deliberation splits, grade them afterwards, and weight perspectives by their record.
29. **[world] E1, when he funds it.** Deliberation against one strong model on frozen cases. If it adds only prose, narrow it.
30. **[think] What transfers.** When a second product exists, measure what carried over (buyer access, research, production, care) against what was rebuilt.
31. **[think] Forgetting.** A record kept for audit is not a record that should steer.

## E. Elastic operation

32. **[code] Loops follow prediction error.** A loop runs more when its subject diverges from what was sealed. The Etsy reader comes first: hourly while a test is live and surprising, daily when quiet.
33. **[think] Set points, not only limits,** for owner-minutes, spend per decision and overhead per asset.
34. **[code] Search terms that retrieve nothing retire themselves.**
35. **[think] Every model call names the decision it could change.** A call that could change none is skipped.
36. **[code] Quiet is a state.** One line on Home when nothing diverges.
37. **[think] Budget elasticity within the charter,** said aloud when used.

## F. Portfolio, correlation and the river

38. **[code] Concentration at four levels:** venue, buyer segment, shared recipe, and traffic source (the handoff). Forty Etsy workbooks are forty SKUs and one platform dependency. A late formula defect touches every sale of one recipe. `null` never reads as "nothing shared".
39. **[think] A diversity target,** set before the second asset, not after.
40. **[think] Correlated-failure runbooks:** the shop suspended, the processor frozen, the model provider down for a week. Each runbook gets a laboratory rehearsal.
41. **[think] Defend before extending, as a scheduler rule.** Threats to live assets are ordered ahead of origination, and the order is shown.
42. **[think] Asset independence:** its own name, identity and failure boundary.
43. **[world] A second asset on an independent channel,** chosen to lower correlation rather than to repeat what worked.
44. **[think] When two small assets are worth more as one.**

## G. Distribution and channels

45. **[think] Reachability is the primary economic variable.** A recipe lowers the cost of supply; it does not make buyers arrive. **A store without traffic is infrastructure, not evidence.**
46. **[code] Acquisition-source labels, only from provider evidence:** `venue_search`, `venue_other`, `owned_site`, `partner`, `paid`, `direct_unknown`, `unresolved` (the handoff). A sale URL alone does not prove its source.
47. **[think] The Workshop as an owned channel:** what consented audience it builds, and what it may say to them (PENDING 18).
48. **[code] A channel scorecard:** cost to be found, conversion, policy risk, what it gives and what it takes, graded from ECONOMICS sources.
49. **[think] Search has a landlord.** Define "helpful at scale" so a page is never made to rank.
50. **[world] Etsy findability, restored and measured:** the first data on whether a marketplace works for this form at all.
51. **[think] Word of mouth, known without surveillance.**

## H. Forms, products and mechanisms

52. **[think] Six product mechanisms, one recipe each, never a universal factory** (the handoff): decision workbook; job record kit; physically proven plan; source-grounded edition; interactive utility; licensed workflow. A second asset may reuse a recipe only once its inputs, promise and oracle are explicit. *One recipe exists: the job review (handoff A5). `template_file` stays unmakeable.*
53. **[think] The originality ladder.** Generic template → role-specific decision instrument → expert-tested workflow → original built design → rights-cleared unique dataset → distribution or licensing relationship. Automation is most valuable **after** a defensible product is identified. A finder that only finds easy-to-generate products floods markets with weak files.
54. **[think] Versioning and after-sale care.** A correction makes a new version and finds the buyers of the old one. It never replaces a promise silently. *Each venue order now records the file it was sold (handoff A3).*
55. **[owner] Widen the exchanges:** free-with-a-role and licensing (PENDING 20).
56. **[think] Is what the institution learned itself a product?**
57. **[think] Acquisition as a form:** buying a small existing asset, and the diligence it would need.

## I. The owner: attention, comprehension, trust

58. **[world] F1: the five-question test, on his own phone.**

60. **[code] The weekly view in five short sets** (the handoff): **earned and settled; buyer obligations; marketplace visibility; work deliberately stopped; one decision that would change the economics.** Detail opens only on request. From the phone he can see a buyer problem and pause new exposure in seconds, while existing remedies stay available. A portfolio that earns $200 with daily support decisions may be worse than one that earns $100 quietly, so money and attention are reported together.
61. **[think] Trust calibrated to the record,** shown beside every recommendation.

63. **[think] Two depths of explanation:** one sentence, then the trace.
64. **[code] A comprehension regression suite.**
65. **[think] Does owning Foundry make him a better allocator?**

## O. From the handoff of 28 September

89. **[think] The asset state machine:** `observed → candidate → designed → tested → approved → live → maintained → paused → retired`. Each transition has a witness and a refusal. Some examples:
    - designed → tested needs an independent oracle, never formulas tested against themselves;
    - approved → live needs a venue readback of the price and the bytes;
    - `paused` stops new exposure and keeps every remedy;
    - `retired` keeps who received which version.
    *Several witnesses exist: sealed designs, the B3 file readback, A3's sold version. The states do not yet exist as one object.*
90. **[think] The catalogue as a graph of customer work.** Complements, substitutes and shared exposure are reported separately. Ten sheets on one search phrase cannibalise each other.
91. **[think] The first coherent line:** inquiry → bid (A01) → supplier quote (A05) → change (A04) → job review (A03) → the next bid. It becomes a bundle only after each part is useful on its own.
92. **[world] Milestones instead of forecasts:**
    - the first external payment and delivery;
    - a second independent customer;
    - repeated months of contribution;
    - covered care;
    - one proven adjacent product;
    - one independent acquisition path;
    - proof of owner absence.

    Each has a denominator, and a way to fail.
93. **[think] The $1,000-a-week arithmetic as a sensitivity instrument, never a forecast.** At an assumed $39 price, $2 per order for refunds and support, and $200 a month common cost, the target needs about 139 sales a month, before taxes and the owner's time (the handoff's arithmetic). It is recomputed from Foundry's own ledger as actuals exist. An imported spreadsheet is never the source of truth. Nobody calls a trajectory proven from one month, one listing or a model's estimate.
94. **[think] A permissioned research method that can run for years.**
    - Each candidate gets a ten-example manual evidence sheet with source, date and what it cannot prove.
    - First-party shop data comes only through authorised paths.
    - Discovery with buyers is opt-in.
    - A finished product is compared blind with the best free alternative.
    - No mass scraping; Etsy's API terms are respected.
95. **[think] Autonomy grows per task, never per portfolio:**
    - offline QA;
    - owner-reviewed publication;
    - instrumented readback;
    - proven order, delivery, refund and message coverage;
    - narrow recurring authority;
    - a measured absence test.

    Each rung is earned by evidence, never by a confidence score.
96. **[think] The binding constraint, asked after each result.** Is it no qualified arrival, an unconvincing offer, an unhelpful product, or incomplete stewardship? The next engineering unit answers that constraint before the product count grows. *Asked in `river/proof-3-candidates/DOSSIER.md`, "What would change this".*

## P. Capital research (added 29 September 2026)

97. **[world] Does anything forecast a 15-minute contract better than its own price?** The research loop runs when the owner begins it. Falsifier: 200 resolved markets with the model's Brier difference not below zero at 95%.
98. **[think] Research is not income, and a simulation is not a sale.** Capital research never reaches Economics, MRR or the product pipeline. A trading **product** (a journal, an audit kit, a fee explainer) is an ordinary venture with buyers, independent of any edge.
99. **[think] Authority for money is per venue, per account, per sum, per date.** Nothing in the research can widen into an order. The execution boundary in `capital/CAPITAL_RESEARCH.md` is a design to review, not a plan.
100. **[think] A legacy system is evidence, not a starting point.** Both old bots reported results that their own arithmetic made up. What came into Foundry was rebuilt and tested against the venue's real responses; nothing was imported as code.

## What would show this strategy wrong

- **Six months with no external event:** no sale, no reachable buyer, no settled real test. The strategy was machinery; cut to the smallest thing that reaches the world.
- **`a` does not fall as assets are added.** The threshold thesis is wrong for this owner.
- **Calibration does not rise across ten or more graded tests.** The learning apparatus is prose.
- **The product count grows faster than the milestones in S92.** Foundry is multiplying files, not assets.
