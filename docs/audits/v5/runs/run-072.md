## Preconditions

Alex has 2 companies on Growth tier. One of the products pivoted and no longer needs Foundry monitoring. Alex wants to remove it. As a non-technical founder, Alex expects the process to be self-explanatory.

## Transcript

### Step 1: Look for company removal

Alex is on the dashboard for Company B (the one to remove). Looks for a "Remove" or "Delete" option.

**Codebase finding:** The sidebar has no "Remove Company" option. The Settings page (accessed via sidebar footer) does not have company removal (grep for "delete" or "remove" in settings.ts found no matches). The Privacy page has "Delete My Data" but Alex may not think to look there for company removal.

**Friction:** Alex is non-technical (comfort 4/10). The mental model is "I want to remove this company from Foundry." The path is Privacy & Data > Delete My Data. Alex would more naturally look in Settings or the product management page. This friction compounds for non-technical users who may interpret "Delete My Data" as a GDPR feature, not a company removal action.

**Result:** FRICTION (HIGH) -- Company removal is not intuitive for non-technical founders. The mental model mismatch between "remove company" and "delete my data" is significant.

### Step 2: Find Privacy & Data

Alex navigates the sidebar and eventually finds Privacy under SYSTEM.

**Codebase finding:** Privacy is the 2nd-to-last item in the sidebar under SYSTEM (layout.ts line 289). It is not prominently placed.

**Result:** FRICTION (LOW) -- Privacy is findable but not prominently placed for this use case.

### Step 3: Read the deletion warning

Alex reads the deletion modal: "permanently schedule deletion of all your product data including metrics, briefings, decisions, and agent logs. This cannot be undone."

**Friction:** Alex may hesitate because the wording implies ALL data is deleted. Alex may want to keep historical data (briefings, decisions) for reference. The binary choice (delete everything vs. keep everything) is particularly anxiety-inducing for non-technical users who are unsure what "agent logs" and "metrics" include.

**Result:** FRICTION (MEDIUM) -- Deletion wording is intimidating for non-technical users. No option to preserve historical data.

### Step 4: Execute deletion

Alex confirms and Company B is scheduled for deletion.

**Codebase finding:** After POST `/privacy/delete`, redirect to `/privacy?success=deletion_scheduled` with "Settings saved successfully" message.

**Friction:** Alex is still on the Privacy page for Company B after deletion. The product switcher still shows Company B. Alex may be confused about whether the deletion worked. The "Settings saved successfully" message is ambiguous.

**Result:** FRICTION (HIGH) -- Post-deletion state is confusing. Company B still appears in the product switcher. Success message does not confirm deletion.

### Step 5: Switch to Company A

Alex switches to Company A using the product switcher.

**Codebase finding:** Product switcher at 2 companies (one pending deletion) still shows both. After the 30-day deletion window, Company B will disappear from the list.

**Cross-company awareness check:** PASS. Switching to Company A shows only Company A's data.

**Result:** PASS -- Boundary maintained. But the zombie Company B in the switcher is confusing.

### Step 6: Cross-company boundary check

Alex verifies Company A data is unaffected by Company B's deletion.

**Cross-company awareness check:** PASS. Company B's scheduled deletion has no effect on Company A's data or agent activity.

**Result:** PASS -- Deletion of one company does not affect another.
