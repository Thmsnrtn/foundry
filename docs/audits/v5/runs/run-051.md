## Preconditions

Riley has 12 active companies on Investor-Ready tier. Wants to review billing breakdown per company, update team settings, and verify that billing is consolidated into a single invoice despite fleet size.

## Transcript

### Step 1: Login and navigate to Settings

Riley logs in, lands on the fleet dashboard showing 12 companies. Clicks "Settings" in the sidebar footer.

**Codebase finding:** `src/routes/dashboard/settings.ts` renders the settings page. `_shared.ts` line 60 loads `allProducts` via `getProductsByOwner(founder.id)` -- all 12 products are fetched in a single query. Product switcher in layout.ts line 182 renders a `<select>` dropdown with all 12 options. At 12 items, the dropdown is still navigable on desktop.

**Result:** PASS -- Settings page loads. Product switcher shows all 12 companies.

### Step 2: Check billing section

Riley scrolls to the billing section to confirm single consolidated invoice for $399/month.

**Codebase finding:** Billing is Stripe-based with a single subscription per founder. `src/services/billing/` handles tier enforcement. The Investor-Ready tier ($399) is a flat rate regardless of company count. There is no per-company billing breakdown visible in the Settings UI.

**Friction:** Riley expects a per-company cost allocation or at least a summary showing "$399/mo for 12 companies = $33.25/company". No such breakdown exists. The billing section shows only the Stripe subscription status and a "Manage Billing" link to the Stripe portal.

**Result:** FRICTION (MEDIUM) -- No per-company cost breakdown for fleet billing. Investor-Ready founders managing 12+ companies cannot demonstrate ROI per company to stakeholders.

### Step 3: Team settings

Riley looks for team/seat management to invite co-founders or operators.

**Codebase finding:** `src/routes/dashboard/team.ts` exists. However, the multi-seat model is limited. There is no granular per-company role assignment -- team members see all companies the founder owns.

**Friction:** No per-company access scoping for team members. Riley cannot grant a VP of Product access to only 3 of the 12 companies. This is a fleet-scale governance gap.

**Result:** FRICTION (HIGH) -- Team members have all-or-nothing access to the entire fleet. No per-company scoping.

### Step 4: Verify product switcher at 12 companies

Riley switches between companies using the header dropdown.

**Codebase finding:** `productSwitcher()` in layout.ts renders a `<select>` with `onchange="this.form.submit()"`. The form POSTs to `/switch-product`, which presumably sets a cookie (`foundry_product` per `_shared.ts` line 100). At 12 options, the native `<select>` is functional but provides no search/filter capability.

**Friction:** At 12 companies, a `<select>` dropdown with no search is manageable but not ideal. At 15 (the stated cap), this becomes cumbersome. No keyboard shortcut to switch products.

**Result:** FRICTION (LOW) -- Product switcher is a bare `<select>` with no search. Adequate at 12, marginal at 15.

### Step 5: Cross-company boundary check

Riley switches from Company A to Company B. Verifies that all dashboard data reflects the new company.

**Codebase finding:** `_shared.ts` resolves the active product via cookie. Every dashboard query uses `ctx.productId` scoped from the cookie. The tenant middleware (`src/middleware/tenant.ts` line 37) validates ownership via `getProductByOwner(productId, founder.id)`. Switching products updates the cookie and all subsequent page loads use the new product ID.

**Cross-company awareness check:** PASS. Product switching correctly scopes all queries. No residual data from the previous company leaks into the new context. The cookie-based approach means a single browser tab always shows one company at a time.

**Result:** PASS -- Product boundary is maintained during switching.
