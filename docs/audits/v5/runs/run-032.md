## Step-by-Step Transcript

1. Jamie logs in. Two products exist. No product cookie set.
2. Dashboard redirects to `/portfolio` which is gated on Investor-Ready (from F031-1).
3. Jamie navigates directly to `/settings` to bypass the gate.
4. Settings loads with first product auto-selected. Jamie sees both products listed.
5. Jamie wants to retire the analytics add-on (second product). Scans Settings page for a retire/archive option.
6. **No retire option.** Products section shows name, GitHub URL, and "View" link. No actions.
7. Jamie navigates to Privacy page (`/privacy`). Sees "Delete My Data" option.
8. Jamie reads the delete description: "Permanently delete your product and all associated data."
9. **FRICTION**: Jamie wants to retire (stop monitoring, keep data for reference), not delete. These are different needs. No retirement option exists.
10. Jamie wonders: if they delete one product, what happens to billing? The privacy page does not mention billing.
11. Jamie considers clicking "Delete My Data" for the analytics product. But wait — which product would be deleted?
12. Jamie checks: the product switcher is not visible on tablet (1024x768). The `_shared.ts` `getLayoutContext` selected the first product (scheduling widget) because no cookie is set.
13. **CRITICAL**: If Jamie clicks delete now, they would delete the scheduling widget (their primary revenue product), not the analytics add-on. The privacy page does not show which product is active.
14. Jamie would need to first switch to the analytics product. But the switcher is only in the dashboard header (which they cannot reach due to the portfolio gate).
15. Jamie is stuck: cannot switch products, cannot retire, deletion targets the wrong product.
16. Jamie looks for a product selection on the Privacy page. **None exists.** The product is determined by the `foundry_product` cookie (unset) or defaults to the first product from `getProductsByOwner`.
17. Jamie tries the Settings Products section — clicking "View" on the analytics product goes to `/products/{id}/audit`. This sets a viewing context but does not change the `foundry_product` cookie.
18. Jamie navigates back to Privacy. The product context is still the first product because "View" does not invoke the `/switch-product` POST endpoint.

## Cross-Company Awareness Check

- **CRITICAL**: The privacy/delete action targets the wrong product (first in DB order, not the one Jamie intends to retire). No product name is shown on the privacy page or in the delete modal.
- **CRITICAL**: No mechanism on the privacy page to select which product to act on.
- **Cookie dependency**: The entire product context system depends on a cookie that is only set via POST to `/switch-product` (from portfolio view or product switcher dropdown). Neither is accessible to Jamie.

## Friction Events

| # | Severity | Category | Description |
|---|----------|----------|-------------|
| F032-1 | CRITICAL | Boundary Confusion | Privacy/delete action targets the wrong product. No product selection on the privacy page, and the default selection (first product) is not what Jamie intends to retire. |
| F032-2 | CRITICAL | Blocking Loop | Cannot switch products because portfolio view is gated and product switcher is only in dashboard header. |
| F032-3 | HIGH | Missing Feature | No company retirement. Only full deletion available. |
| F032-4 | MEDIUM | Tablet Layout | Product switcher may not be visible or usable at 1024x768 tablet viewport. |

## Verdict

**BOUNDARY_CONFUSION**

Jamie would delete the wrong company's data because the product selection mechanism is broken for Growth tier multi-product users. The privacy page acts on a phantom product context with no visible indicator of which company is affected.

## Recommendation

1. The privacy/delete page MUST display the product name prominently: "You are managing data for: **Analytics Add-On**".
2. Add a product selector directly on the privacy page.
3. Decouple product switching from the portfolio route — add it to the header of all dashboard pages.
4. Add a retirement flow distinct from deletion.
