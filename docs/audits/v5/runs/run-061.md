## Preconditions

Robin previously had 2 companies but already removed one. Now wants to retire the remaining company from Foundry before cancelling the subscription. Already exported most data manually. Expects a clean, transparent exit.

## Transcript

### Step 1: Find the company retirement/deletion flow

Robin looks for how to remove a company from Foundry.

**Codebase finding:** The sidebar has "Settings" at the bottom (layout.ts line 320) and "Privacy" under SYSTEM (line 289). There is no explicit "Delete Company" or "Retire Company" link in the sidebar navigation. The Privacy page (privacy.ts line 272-285) has a "Request Account Deletion" section that says "Delete My Data" -- but this is framed as data deletion, not company retirement.

**Friction:** Robin's abandonment trigger #1: "Cannot find the cancellation/deletion flow within 30 seconds of looking." The retirement flow is buried in Privacy & Data settings, labeled as "Delete My Data" rather than "Remove Company" or "Retire Company." The mental model mismatch between "privacy settings" and "company removal" adds friction.

**Result:** FRICTION (HIGH) -- Company retirement flow is hidden in Privacy settings, not in a product management or settings section where Robin would naturally look.

### Step 2: Review what deletion means

Robin clicks "Delete My Data" button and reads the confirmation modal.

**Codebase finding:** privacy.ts lines 291-310 show the modal:
- Text: "permanently schedule deletion of all your product data including metrics, briefings, decisions, and agent logs. This cannot be undone."
- 30-day delayed deletion via `scheduleDataDeletion(ctx.productId, 30)`.

**Friction:** Robin wants to know: (1) What happens to agent-generated PRs and branches in GitHub? (2) Is the deletion "soft" (hidden but retained) or "hard" (actually removed)? (3) Is there a 30-day cancellation window?

The modal does not address PRs/branches in GitHub repos. The word "schedule" implies a delay but does not confirm whether Robin can reverse the decision within 30 days. Robin's abandonment trigger #3: "No confirmation of what happens to agent-generated artifacts (PRs, commits, branches) post-deletion."

**Result:** FRICTION (CRITICAL) -- Deletion confirmation does not address: (1) agent-generated GitHub artifacts, (2) whether deletion is reversible within 30 days, (3) what "scheduled deletion" means in practice.

### Step 3: Execute deletion

Robin confirms deletion. POST `/privacy/delete` is submitted.

**Codebase finding:** privacy.ts line 406-416 calls `scheduleDataDeletion(ctx.productId, 30)` and redirects to `/privacy?success=deletion_scheduled`. The success message handling (line 89-93) shows a generic "Settings saved successfully" banner -- it does not show a deletion-specific confirmation.

**Friction:** After deletion, the redirect shows "Settings saved successfully" which is incorrect for a deletion action. Robin expects: "Deletion scheduled. All data will be permanently removed by [date]. You will receive a confirmation email."

**Result:** FRICTION (HIGH) -- Post-deletion feedback is a generic "Settings saved" message, not a deletion-specific confirmation with timeline.

### Step 4: Verify subscription cancellation

Robin wants to cancel the Growth subscription now that the last company is being deleted.

**Codebase finding:** The settings page links to Stripe customer portal for billing management. Cancellation happens through Stripe, not within Foundry. There is no in-app cancellation flow.

**Friction:** Robin must leave Foundry and navigate the Stripe portal to cancel. There is no connection between "delete my product data" and "cancel my subscription." Robin's abandonment trigger #2: "If I have to email support to delete my account, you've already lost the exit interview too." While Robin doesn't need to email support, the two-step process (delete data in Foundry, cancel billing in Stripe) is fragmented.

**Result:** FRICTION (MEDIUM) -- No unified exit flow. Data deletion and subscription cancellation are separate workflows in different systems.
