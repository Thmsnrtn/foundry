## Step-by-Step Transcript

1. Sam logs in. Portfolio view shows 5 companies. Company E has Signal 45 — it is the "winding down" product.
2. Sam clicks Company E. Dashboard loads. Sam wants to retire this company.
3. Sam navigates to Settings (`/settings`). Products section lists all 5 products. No per-product retirement action.
4. Sam navigates to Privacy (`/privacy`). Sees "Delete My Data" for the current product.
5. Sam reads the delete description carefully. "Permanently delete your product and all associated data."
6. **FRICTION**: Sam wants to archive Company E, not delete it. Sam wants to keep 3 months of historical data for reference and board packets.
7. Sam considers: can they just stop the SCP agents without deleting data? Checks Agents page (`/agents`).
8. Agents page shows the 12 SCP agents for Company E with their health status. No "pause all" or "stop" option. Individual agents cannot be paused from the UI.
9. Sam checks the SCP scheduler. It runs hourly via cron job. No per-product enable/disable flag visible.
10. **FRICTION**: No way to stop SCP agents for a specific company. The scheduler runs for all products with active SCP instances.
11. Sam navigates back to Privacy. Clicks "Delete My Data." Modal appears.
12. **Sam notices**: the modal does not name Company E. It says "all your product data." Sam double-checks the product switcher in the header — it shows "Company E" selected. Correct.
13. Sam clicks "Yes, Delete Everything." Deletion scheduled.
14. **Redirect with generic success message.** Sam expected a confirmation with a timeline.
15. Sam navigates to portfolio. **Company E still shows in the portfolio grid.** Signal still computing. SCP agents still running.
16. **FRICTION**: No immediate visual change after scheduling deletion. Company appears fully active.
17. Sam wonders: will billing decrease? At $399/month Investor-Ready, the pricing is flat per tier, not per company. Retiring one company does not change the bill.
18. **FRICTION**: No billing adjustment for retiring a company. The journey expects per-company billing adjustment, but Foundry uses flat tier pricing.
19. After 30 days (simulated): Company E data deleted. Product status becomes `deleted`. But product still appears in `getProductsByOwner` results unless filtered by status.
20. **FRICTION**: Need to verify that `getProductsByOwner` filters out `status='deleted'` products. If not, the portfolio view would show a ghost card with no data.

## Cross-Company Awareness Check

- **Product selection**: Company E was correctly selected via cookie. The delete action targeted the right company.
- **Portfolio impact**: After deletion, the portfolio should show 4 companies. But during the 30-day grace period, Company E remains visible with stale data.
- **Fleet aggregation**: If cross-company metrics existed, retiring a company should exclude it from aggregations. This is not tested because cross-company intelligence does not exist.

## Friction Events

## Verdict

**FAILED**

Company retirement does not exist. Deletion is the only option, and it destroys all historical data after 30 days with no intermediate archive state. Sam cannot stop monitoring without deleting data, and billing does not adjust.

## Recommendation

1. Add a product lifecycle state machine: Active -> Paused -> Archived -> Deleted.
2. "Paused" stops SCP agents but keeps data accessible. "Archived" makes data read-only with a retention policy.
3. Add a per-product toggle in Settings or Agents to pause SCP execution.
4. Consider per-company add-on pricing for Investor-Ready tier ($399 base + $X per company) so retirement has a billing benefit.
