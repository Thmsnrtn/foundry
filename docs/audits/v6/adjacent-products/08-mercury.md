# Mercury — Banking + Financial OS for Startups

**Category:** E — Startup OS
**Last researched:** 2026-04-18

## What It Does

Mercury is a fintech company providing digital banking for startups: no-fee checking
and savings accounts, treasury management (auto-sweep into money market funds at 4-5%
APY), corporate credit cards (IO card, 1.5% cashback), bill pay, expense management,
and financial automation. 200,000+ customers, $500M revenue in 2024, $3.5B valuation.
Applied for its own national bank charter in December 2025 — signaling a move from
fintech wrapper to full banking entity.

## Target User

Primary: startup founders and early finance hires at seed through Series C companies.
Secondary: small and medium businesses needing modern banking without legacy bank
friction. Mercury's positioning is explicitly "for startups" — the brand, UX, and
feature set assume a founder audience.

## Positioning (How They Describe Themselves)

"Online business banking for startups, small businesses & scaling companies." Mercury
frames itself as a complete financial platform, not just a bank account — banking,
treasury, cards, and automation in one interface. The implicit promise: "everything
financial, no legacy bank nonsense."

## Pricing

- Core banking: Free (no monthly fees, no minimums, no domestic wire/ACH fees)
- Plus: $35/mo (advanced accounting integrations, enhanced features)
- Pro: $350/mo (NetSuite integration, higher-volume expense management)
- IO credit card: No annual fee, no personal guarantee
- Revenue model: interchange fees, treasury management fees, interest margin

## Key Vocabulary

Checking, savings, treasury, sweep, wire, ACH, bill pay, expense management,
approval flow, rule, automation, FDIC insurance, API, webhook, transaction
categorization, corporate card, cashback, financial automation.

## Onboarding Flow

Streamlined digital onboarding: provide formation documents and EIN, 5-7 day
approval process. No branch visits, no paper forms. First banking features
available immediately after approval. Treasury, cards, and advanced features
activated progressively. The entire experience is designed for "done in 1-2
minutes within the app."

## Multi-Entity Handling

Mercury does NOT support multi-entity management as of 2026. Each company requires
a separate Mercury account. There is no consolidated view across entities, no
inter-company transfers, no fleet-level treasury management. This is a significant
limitation for multi-company operators — the exact persona Foundry targets.

## Agent Handling

Mercury offers AI-powered transaction categorization and rule-based automation
(e.g., "sweep balances above $100K into treasury every Friday"). These are smart
automation features, not autonomous agents. No decision-making, no proactive
intelligence, no agent coordination. Mercury automates banking operations, not
business operations.

## Novelty Positioning

Category familiarity with UX differentiation. "Banking for startups" is not a new
category — Mercury competes with traditional banks and other neobanks (Brex, Rho,
Relay). The differentiation is execution: better UX, better API, better integrations,
zero-fee structure. The bank charter application signals a move toward owning the
category rather than wrapping it.

## What Foundry Can Learn

1. **Free core + paid power features as a wedge.** Mercury's free banking is a
   powerful wedge — founders adopt it because there's no reason not to. Foundry's
   solo tier ($79) competes with "do nothing" (spreadsheets, manual ops). A free
   tier with limited SCP capability could dramatically lower adoption friction.

2. **Multi-entity gap as a market signal.** Mercury's lack of multi-entity support,
   despite serving 200K+ customers, validates that multi-company operators are
   underserved. If Mercury — a $3.5B company — hasn't built this, the problem is
   genuinely hard and genuinely needed. Foundry's fleet management fills a gap that
   even well-funded fintech hasn't addressed.

3. **"Everything financial, one interface" as the Startup OS template.** Mercury
   earned "OS" status by collapsing banking, treasury, cards, and automation into
   one experience. Foundry's "OS" claim requires similar collapse — all the
   intelligence, monitoring, decision support, and execution for running a company
   in one interface. The bar is comprehensive but seamless.
