# Bid Decision Workbook — how to use it

Thank you for buying this. Here is everything you need, in five minutes.

## What it does

You log every bid you consider on the **Bids** sheet. The workbook works out, from your own history, how often you win from each lead source, what a bid is worth once you allow for that, and whether the hours it takes to bid are worth it. The **Summary** sheet shows win rate by lead source, why you lose, and how much submitted work is sitting against the capacity you set.

It does not price or estimate a job, write proposals or invoices, schedule work, or track costs on a job. One file for one decision: bid or don't.

## Set it up

1. Open **Settings**. Enter your bidding hourly rate (what an hour of your time spent bidding is worth to you), the monthly capacity you can take on in dollars, and a starting guess at your win rate for use until you have history. Edit the lead sources and loss reasons if yours are different. Yellow cells with blue text are yours; everything else is formulas.
2. Open **Bids**. Delete the grey EXAMPLE rows. Enter your own bids, one per row: client, scope in one line, lead source (drop-down), date received, due date, estimated value, hours it will take to bid, status (drop-down), and when it is submitted or decided, those dates. If you lost, pick a loss reason.
3. Read the **Signal** column before you spend hours on a bid. "Worth it", "Thin" or "Skip?" is a signal, not an instruction; you decide.
4. Read **Summary** once a month.

## How the Signal is worked out

- Win rate for a lead source is won divided by (won plus lost) from your own rows, once that source has at least the number of decided bids set on Settings (three by default). Before that it uses your overall win rate, and before you have any history it uses the starting guess on Settings.
- Expected value = estimated value × win rate.
- Bid cost = hours to bid × your rate.
- The Signal compares expected value per bidding hour with your rate: "Worth it" at or above the multiple you set on Settings, "Thin" at or above your rate, "Skip?" below it.
